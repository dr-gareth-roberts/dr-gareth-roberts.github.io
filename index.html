01 — Approach

I study how intelligence works — in cortical networks and in transformers.

My PhD and two postdoctoral fellowships were in cognitive neuroscience: the neural mechanisms by which the prefrontal and frontoparietal systems convert verbal instructions into goal-directed action — attention, working memory, cognitive control, and how they fail under complexity. The work spans experimental psychology, EEG/fMRI/fNIRS neuroimaging and the learning sciences, with peer-reviewed publications and invited talks across Australia, the UK and Europe.

For the last several years I've built production ML inside regulated industries — insurance, mining, HR-tech — where a model isn't done when it's accurate; it's done when it's governed, auditable and trusted. I've founded AI functions from scratch, led engineering teams, and shipped multi-agent systems into environments where the regulator is a stakeholder. The throughline is the same question I studied in the brain: how do intelligent systems represent instructions, hold goals, and stay reliable?

Research themes

03 — Research
01

Cognitive control & task instructions

How the prefrontal and frontoparietal systems convert verbal instructions into abstract motor plans — goal maintenance, goal neglect, and the role of working memory.

02

Learning sciences

Productive failure, analogical comparison and far transfer; learning about complex systems. Highly-cited work in Learning & Instruction.

03

Neuroimaging methods

EEG, fMRI and fNIRS — resting-state connectivity, time-frequency and measure-projection analysis. Founded two neuromethods groups and built lab capability from scratch.

04

Human & artificial intelligence

Bringing the cognitive science of attention, memory and control to bear on how language models reason, are evaluated, and can be made reliable and safe.
